How to Get Your Child Excited About Reading: A Parent's Guide to Building a Lifelong Love of Books

Every parent dreams of seeing their child curled up with a good book, completely lost in a world of imagination and wonder. But in a world full of screens, apps, and instant entertainment, getting kids excited about reading can sometimes feel like an uphill battle. The good news? With a few simple, fun strategies, you can spark a genuine love of reading in your child — one that will benefit them for the rest of their life. Whether your little one is just learning their ABCs or already working through chapter books, this guide is packed with warm, practical tips to make reading the best part of their day.

1. Start With Stories They Already Love

The fastest way to get a child excited about reading is to meet them where their interests already are. Does your child love dinosaurs, unicorns, superheroes, or silly jokes? There is a children's book for absolutely everything — and finding stories that connect to their existing passions makes reading feel like a reward rather than a chore.

Visit your local library or browse an online children's book collection and let your child take the lead. When kids feel like they have a say in what they read, they instantly become more invested. Ask them questions like, "Would you rather read about a dragon who breathes ice cream or a dog who goes to school?" Giving them choices builds ownership over their reading journey and gets them genuinely excited to find out what happens next.

2. Make Read-Aloud Time a Special Ritual

There is something truly magical about reading aloud together. Research consistently shows that children who are read to regularly develop stronger vocabularies, better comprehension skills, and — most importantly — a deeper love for books. But beyond the academic benefits, read-aloud time is a precious bonding experience that your child will remember long into adulthood.

Create a cozy reading ritual that your child looks forward to every day. Set up a dedicated reading nook with soft pillows and good lighting. Choose a consistent time — right before bed is a classic favourite — and stick to it so it becomes a comforting part of the daily routine. Use fun voices for different characters, add dramatic pauses, and don't be afraid to be a little silly. When reading feels like a performance and a shared adventure, kids naturally start asking for "just one more chapter."

3. Let Them See You Reading Too

Children are incredible little observers, and they model their behaviour on the adults they admire most. If your child sees you reaching for a book instead of your phone during quiet moments, that sends a powerful message: reading is something grown-ups enjoy too.

Talk about what you're reading at the dinner table. Share funny moments from a book you loved as a child. Visit the library together and each pick something out. When reading becomes a family value rather than a school assignment, children absorb that attitude naturally. You don't have to make a big deal out of it — just let them see that books are a natural, enjoyable part of everyday life in your home.

4. Celebrate Progress and Keep It Pressure-Free

One of the biggest obstacles to children loving reading is turning it into something stressful. If every reading session comes with corrections, comprehension quizzes, or frustration about pace, kids will start to associate books with anxiety rather than joy. The goal at home is to nurture enthusiasm, not perfect performance.

Celebrate every milestone, no matter how small. Did your child sound out a tricky word? Cheer! Did they finish their first full book independently? Mark it with something special — a bookmark they get to keep, a little treat, or simply lots of enthusiastic praise. You might also try keeping a simple "books we've read" list on the fridge where they can add stickers for each book completed. Visual progress is incredibly motivating for young readers, and it turns reading into an adventure they're actively tracking.

Remember, it's also completely okay to abandon a book that isn't clicking. Forcing a child through a story they dislike is a quick way to kill their enthusiasm. Let them move on and try something new — finding the right book at the right time is part of the journey.

5. Explore Different Types of Books and Formats

Reading doesn't have to mean sitting quietly with a traditional picture book or novel. The world of children's literature is wonderfully diverse, and exploring different formats can reignite excitement in kids who feel like reading "isn't for them."

Try graphic novels and comic-style books, which are fantastic for reluctant readers because the visual storytelling makes comprehension more accessible and enjoyable. Explore non-fiction books about topics they're passionate about — many children who don't gravitate toward fiction will devour an illustrated book about sharks, space, or how things are made. Don't overlook children's cookbooks either, which are a brilliant and often overlooked reading tool. Following a recipe builds reading comprehension, sequencing skills, and real-world confidence — all while creating something delicious together in the kitchen.

Audiobooks are another wonderful gateway. Listening to a well-narrated story on a road trip or during quiet time can introduce children to books they might not have attempted on their own, and often inspires them to seek out the physical book afterward.

6. Build a Home Library They're Proud Of

There is something genuinely exciting about having your own collection of books. Even a small shelf filled with titles your child has chosen and loves creates a sense of pride and ownership. When children feel like their books belong to them — not just borrowed from a classroom or library — they're more likely to return to them again and again.

You don't need to spend a fortune to build a wonderful home library. Second-hand bookshops, library sales, and online children's book stores offer incredible value. Let your child help arrange and organise their books. Encourage them to recommend favourites to friends and family. The act of being a "book recommender" is empowering for kids and reinforces their identity as a reader — which is exactly the mindset you want to nurture.

Your Child's Reading Adventure Starts Today

Getting your child excited about reading isn't about finding one magic trick — it's about creating an environment where books feel joyful, personal, and utterly worth their time. Start small, stay consistent, follow their lead, and celebrate every step of the journey. The habits and love of reading you help build today will open doors for your child for the rest of their life. And remember: the best book for your child is simply the one they can't wait to open.
