Why children's books on kindness work

Children's books on kindness give kids a front-row seat to someone else's feelings. That is the first step to caring about them.

Making Caring Common, a project at the Harvard Graduate School of Education, suggests using stories to build empathy. Ask about how the characters feel, and ask about more than one character, not just the hero. With older kids, ask how they would feel in that character's place.

The same group encourages parents to make caring for others a priority, and to tell children that being kind matters as much as being happy. Try "be kind and be happy" instead of only "be happy."

Schools call this work social and emotional learning. CASEL, a leading group in that field, includes learning to understand and manage emotions, to feel and show empathy for others, and to build healthy relationships.

Questions to ask while you read

You do not need a lesson plan. A few questions in the middle of the story are enough.

  • "How does she feel right now?"Point to a face in the picture. ZERO TO THREE suggests asking which child looks mad or which looks afraid.
  • "What about him?"Ask about a side character too, not just the main one. Kids learn that everyone in a story has feelings.
  • "What would you do?"Pause before a character makes a choice and let your child decide first.
  • "Has that ever happened to you?"Connect the story to your child's own day at school or church.

Children's books about feelings

Kindness is easier when a child can name what they feel. A child who knows the word "worried" can say it instead of acting it out. Books like The Worry Cloud in My Tummy and Conscious Discipline: Understanding Our Feelings put a name on a feeling right in the title.

Sharing, the hard part of kindness

For young children, sharing is often the hardest kind of kindness. Read a sharing story before a playdate or a family gathering, and talk about one thing your child could share. Then notice and praise it when it happens.

Kindness and faith

For Christian families, kindness is part of following Jesus. Two verses to learn together:

"And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."

Ephesians 4:32 (KJV)

"Therefore all things whatsoever ye would that men should do to you, do ye even so to them: for this is the law and the prophets."

Matthew 7:12 (KJV)
