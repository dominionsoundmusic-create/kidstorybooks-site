Why Christian children's books belong at home

Christian children's books give families an easy way to talk about God at the kitchen table, in the car and at bedtime. That is where much of a child's faith is shaped.

Many church leaders say the same. In a Barna Group study done with Cardus, nearly all Protestant pastors and Catholic priests ranked parents first, ahead of the church, as responsible for children's faith formation. In an earlier Barna survey, most parents of children under 13 said that job was theirs, too.

Research from the Pew Research Center points the same way. A religious upbringing often leads to a religious adulthood, though not always. Adults who grew up in homes where faith was talked about a lot were more likely to keep their parents' faith than adults whose parents rarely discussed it.

A book makes those talks easier. You do not have to plan a lesson. You read the story, and the questions come on their own.

Tips for reading faith books together

  • Read it twiceNAEYC, the national early childhood group, tells families to reread children's favorites.
  • Find the verseIf a book names a verse, open your Bible and read it together. Let your child put a finger on the words.
  • Pray about the storyEnd with a one-line prayer about what you read: "Thank you, God, for taking care of us like you did for Noah."
  • Bring in grandparentsGrandparents can read over a video call or keep a few books at their house for visits.
