What makes good picture books for kindergarteners

Picture books for kindergarteners can do more than books for toddlers. At five and six, children can follow a plot with a problem and a fix, guess why a character feels a certain way, and remember a story from one day to the next.

Reading Rockets, a national literacy project, suggests reading a rich picture book aloud every day, along with predictable books that repeat lines. As children grow, add true information books with photos about the world.

Words matter, too. Researchers at Ohio State estimated that children whose parents read them five books a day hear about 1.4 million more words by kindergarten than children who are never read to. The lead researcher said the real gap is likely even bigger.

How to read aloud to a five or six year old

Do not just read the words. Talk about the book. Reading Rockets notes that research shows this kind of "dialogic reading," where the adult asks questions and the child helps tell the story, builds oral language and vocabulary.

  • Ask about the pictureThe CDC suggests asking "What is happening in the picture?" or "What do you think will happen next?" and then asking for more details.
  • Read it againNAEYC tells families to reread children's favorites. Each time, your child notices something new.
  • Pick a few wordsTeachers often choose a handful of new words to explain while reading. Point to the picture and say what the word means.
  • Let them retell itBy age 5, most children can tell a story with at least two events. Ask them to tell it back to you.

Why animal stories work so well at this age

Animal characters let children try on big feelings from a safe distance. A story animal can be worried, proud or embarrassed, and a child can talk about that feeling without talking about themselves. A title like The Shark Who Needed Glasses or The Library Lion Who Forgot How to Roar starts that kind of talk before you even open the cover.

Animal books also lead to real-world questions. "Do sharks really have teeth that fall out?" is a great time to find an information book together. Our nature books for kids page has life cycle books on frogs, ladybugs, sea turtles and more.

Picture books about friendship and family

Kindergarten is often a child's first big group. Stories about brothers, sisters, neighbors and friends give you a way to talk about sharing and taking turns. Titles on the children's shelf include Kofi and Kwame: Brothers Through Thick and Thin, The Butterfly Sisters and The New Little Sister. For more, see children's books on kindness.
