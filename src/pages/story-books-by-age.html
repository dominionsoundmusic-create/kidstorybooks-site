How to choose story books for 3 year olds

Story books for 3 year olds should be short, bright and easy to join in on. At this age, most of the fun is in hearing the same book again and pointing at the pictures.

The CDC's milestone checklist says that by age 4, most children can tell what comes next in a story they know well. Three is when that starts, so pick books your child will want to hear over and over.

One honest note: most of the children's stories on this site do not state an age in the title, and the site never guesses one. The books listed below are the ones whose titles say an age. For everything else, use the guide on this page and the topic pages to find a good fit.

A simple age-by-age guide

  • BabiesThe AAP suggests chunky board books, soft cloth books or vinyl bath books from birth. Chewing on books is normal and part of how babies learn about them.
  • Toddlers and 3 year oldsBoard books and short picture books with rhymes and lines that repeat. Let them turn the pages and point.
  • 4 and 5 year oldsPicture books with a real problem and a fix. Ask what comes next and let them finish familiar lines.
  • 6 to 8 year oldsA U.S. Department of Education guide suggests adding books made for beginning readers, including some with chapters, by early first grade.
  • 9 and 10 year oldsLonger stories, series and nonfiction about their interests. Keep a read-aloud book going too.
  • TeensLet them choose. See our books for teens page for ideas on keeping older kids reading.

Keep reading aloud after they can read

It is easy to stop reading aloud once a child can read alone. Reading Rockets says that is a mistake: reading together is still important, and reading stories that match your child's interests but sit above their reading level stretches their understanding and makes them want to improve.

Scholastic's Kids & Family Reading Report found that reading aloud at home peaks around age five and drops off with each year after that. The AAP also points out that children often come to link reading with schoolwork, so reading for fun at home matters.

Story books for 6, 7 and 8 year olds

This is the age of "I can read it myself!" Let them read a page, then you read a page. Picture books with a full plot still have a place, even for confident readers.

Short story books for 10 year olds

Older kids often want something they can finish fast. Short books, nonfiction about a favorite subject and books with a clear, strong theme work well. Let your child pick from a few choices.
