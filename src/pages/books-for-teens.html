Why books for teens are worth the push

Finding books for teens is harder than it used to be. Phones, school and sports all compete for the same few free hours.

The numbers show it. In Common Sense Media's 2019 survey, just over half of teens read for fun at least once a week, and almost a third read for fun less than once a month, if at all. Only 24% of teens said they enjoy reading "a lot," compared with 38% of tweens.

Federal testing data tell the same story. The Nation's Report Card has tracked 13 year olds for decades, and fewer of them say they read for fun almost every day now than in the 1980s.

Reading still matters at this age. The Young Adult Library Services Association, part of the American Library Association, says books written for young adults meet teens' developmental needs, including their reading skills.

Tips to keep teens reading

  • Let them pickOffer a few choices and let the final call be theirs.
  • Use the libraryYALSA points to a 2010 Dominican University study: students in a public library summer reading program scored higher on reading tests the next fall.
  • Read the same bookRead it too, then talk about it over a meal. No quiz, just talk.
