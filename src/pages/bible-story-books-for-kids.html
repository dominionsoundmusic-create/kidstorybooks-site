Bible stories for preschoolers start with a few favorites

Bible stories for preschoolers work best when they are short, clear and told again and again. A 3 or 4 year old does not need every detail. They need one picture to hold onto: a boat full of animals, a big fish, a boy with a sling.

Repeating the same story is not a waste of time. ZERO TO THREE, a national early childhood group, says young children learn through repetition and need to hear the same story many times to fully understand it. Adults may tire of a book long before a child does.

Many parents also wonder how much a preschooler can really take in. The CDC's milestone checklists give a helpful guide. These are things most children (about three out of four) can do by each age.

What to expect at 3, 4 and 5

  • By age 3Most children ask "who," "what," "where" and "why" questions and can keep up a short back-and-forth talk. Expect questions like "Why did God tell Noah to build a boat?"
  • By age 4Most children can tell what comes next in a story they know well. Pause before the big moment and let them say it: "And then the fish..."
  • By age 5Most children can tell a story with at least two events and answer simple questions about a book after you read it. Ask them to tell the story back to you.

A list of Bible stories for preschoolers

Here are ten stories many families start with. Read the passage in your own Bible first, then tell or read it to your child in simple words.

  1. Creation, Genesis 1 and 2
  2. Noah and the ark, Genesis 6 to 9
  3. The tower of Babel, Genesis 11
  4. Baby Moses, Exodus 2
  5. Crossing the Red Sea, Exodus 14
  6. David and Goliath, 1 Samuel 17
  7. Elijah and the widow's son, 1 Kings 17
  8. Jonah and the big fish, Jonah 1 and 2
  9. The birth of Jesus, Luke 2
  10. Jesus and the children, Mark 10

How to read a Bible story with a young child

Use a book with big pictures and stop to talk about them. Reading experts call this kind of back-and-forth "dialogic reading," and Reading Rockets notes that research shows it builds oral language and vocabulary. Ask "What do you see?" and "What do you think happens next?" Then let your child help tell the story.

When questions come up that you cannot answer, it is fine to say "Let's find out together" and look in the Bible with your child.
